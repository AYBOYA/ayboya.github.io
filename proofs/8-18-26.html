[Linear Algebra] Span of a Subset of a Vector Space is a Subspace

8-18-26

Reccomended listening: Chopin "Revolutionary" Etude - Evgeny Kissin

Before the proof, remember a theorem:

A subset (S) of a Vector Space (V) is a Subspace iff, for \(x,y \in S\), scalar \(c\):
  1. \(0_V \in S\)
  2. \(x+y \in S\)
  3. \(cx \in S\)
PROOF
Consider a subset, S, of a vector space, V.

By cases;

If \(S = \emptyset\): notice \(span(S) = \{0\}\).
\(\{0\}\) is a subspace

If \(S \neq \emptyset\):
\(\implies \exists z \in S\)
\(0z = 0 \in span(S)\) - Satisfying the first condition

\(\forall x,y \in span(S), \exists \) vectors \(u_1,u_2...u_n\) and \(v_1,v_2...v_n \in S\)
and scalars \(a_1,a_2...a_n\) and \(b_1,b_2...b_n\)
such that:
\(x = a_1u_1 + a_2u_2 + ... + a_nu_n\)
\(y = b_1v_1 + b_2v_2 + ... + b_nv_n\)

Then,
\(x + y = a_1u_1 + a_2u_2 + ... + a_nu_n + b_1v_1 + b_2v_2 + ... + b_nv_n \in span(S)\) - Satisfying the second condition

Finally, introducing some scalar, \(c\),
\(cx = ca_1u_1 + ca_2u_2 + ... + ca_nu_n \in span(S)\) - Satisfying the third condition
DONE
