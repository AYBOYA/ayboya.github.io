Relationship between inverses, surjectivity, and injectivity

Reccomended listening: Rachmaninoff Piano Concerto No. 3 - Yuncham Lim

To begin, a set of essential relationships between left/right inverses and injectivity/surjectivity.

Proposition 1.1: The map \(f\) is injective \(\iff f\) has a left inverse

PROOF
let \(a,b,x\in A\)

\("\implies"\)
\(f:A\to B\) is injective \(\iff a \neq b \implies f(a) \neq f(b)\)

let \(a_0 \in A\)

define \(h:B \to A\) as \(z \in B \to \begin{cases} x & \text{if } z \in Range(f), \\ a_0 & \text{if } z \notin Range(f). \end{cases}\)

\(h\) is well defined, as:
  1. \(\forall f(x) \in B, \exists x\)
  2. \(a,b \in A, f(x)\to a \cap f(x)\to b \implies a = b\) because \(f\) is injective

Finally, \(h\circ f\) has a mapping \(x \to f(x) \to x\) (identity function on A)
So \(h\) is a left inverse of \(f\)

\("\impliedby"\)
\(g:A \to B\)
\(j:\) left inverse of \(g\)

Sps. \(g\) is not injective
\(\implies \exists a,b \in A, a\neq b \cap g(a) = g(b)\)
Notice the mapping of \(j\) must include \(g(a) \to a\) AND \(g(a) \to b\)
\(j\) fails to be well defined
\(\bot\)
DONE

Proposition 1.2: The map \(f\) is surjective \(\iff f\) has a right inverse

PROOF
\("\implies"\)
\(f:A\to B\) is surjective \(\iff \forall b \in B \exists a \in A, f(a) \to b\)

for \(x\in B\) let \(w_x = \{z \in A | f(z) = x\}\), which is nonempty because \(f\) is surjective

define \(h:B \to A\) as \(f(x) \to\) some element in \(w_x\)

\(h\) is well defined, as:
  1. \(\forall f(x) \in B, \exists x\) because \(f\) is surjective
  2. \(a,b \in A, f(x)\to a \cap f(x)\to b \implies a = b\) because each \(w_x\) is nonoverlapping

Finally, \(f\circ h\) has a mapping \(f(x) \to q \to f(x)\) where \(q \in w_x\) (identity function on B)
So \(h\) is a right inverse of \(f\)

\("\impliedby"\)
\(g:A \to B\)
\(j:\) right inverse of \(g\)

Sps. \(g\) is not surjective
\(\implies \exists b \in B, \nexists a \in A, g(a) = b\)
Notice the mapping of \(j\) does not map \(b\) to any element
\(j\) fails to be well defined
\(\bot\)
DONE

Proposition 1.3: The map \(f\) is bijective \(\iff f\) has a function that is both a left AND right inverse

PROOF
\("\implies"\)
\(f\) bijective \(\implies f\) surjective and injective
\(\implies \exists\) right inverse \(p\) and left inverse \(q\)

Notice that the constructions for the left and right inverses in the above proofs are equivalent functions if \(f\) is both injective and surjective

In other words, \(p=q\) is both a left AND right inverse of \(f\)

\("\impliedby"\)
\(f\) has a left and right inverse

\(\implies f\) is injective and surjective
\(\implies f\) is bijective
DONE
